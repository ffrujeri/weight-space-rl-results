WEIGHT-SPACE PROGRAMMING · CONTROL PILOT

Astra vs SAC from random initialization

Three environments. Identical initial actors. Matched online interaction budgets.

Completed experiments · initialization/training seed 7 · 100 held-out episodes per policy

What this pilot shows. A reasoning model can construct useful controllers directly inside a randomly initialized neural network. Walker2d is the strongest result; Pendulum is effectively tied, and Hopper mainly stands. This is a feasibility result with substantial physics knowledge, not evidence that weight editing generally outperforms reinforcement learning.

Held-out performance

Higher return is better. Astra and SAC received exactly the same random actor tensors, verified by SHA-256 fingerprints. Astra then directly assigned weights without gradients; SAC trained normally on GPU. No trained policy checkpoint was supplied to Astra.

Training and development curves

The top panels show SAC's exploratory training returns, including a trailing mean over 10 episodes. The bottom panels show Astra's deterministic candidate feedback and the best controller available after each feedback block. Candidate 0 is the initial random policy; every unsuccessful candidate remains visible. These traces use different feedback protocols and are not matched held-out learning curves.

Astra consumed 4,096 initial-policy transitions plus 4,096 per candidate, including partial episodes. It stopped after two candidates on Pendulum and eight on each MuJoCo task. SAC trained to 36,864 steps on all tasks; the Pendulum comparison uses only its saved 12,288-step checkpoint. The gray region shows later SAC training excluded from that matched comparison.

What the policies do

  • Pendulum: an energy-shaping controller plus upright proportional/derivative control. The held-out difference from SAC is −1.80, with a paired interval that includes zero.
  • Hopper: mostly static standing, without sustained hopping. Astra survives all 1,000 steps on every test episode; SAC averages 333.16 steps. Healthy-survival reward explains much of Astra's return.
  • Walker2d: slow alternating/shuffling forward motion, about 0.42 m/s in development. Astra survives all 1,000 steps on every test episode; SAC averages 378.07. The speed is a development observation, not a separately measured held-out speed metric.

Protocol and interpretation

The actor is CleanRL SAC's native network: two 256-unit ReLU layers, mean and log-standard-deviation heads, tanh squashing, and fixed action bounds. All selected Astra candidates replace every actor parameter with explicitly designed control circuits. The architecture and environment remain fixed.

Astra received raw trajectories, observation/action semantics, and native environment Python. The Hopper and Walker2d model XMLs were also supplied on request. Physics and classical-control knowledge supplied the designs; feedback informed sign corrections and other numerical design choices. No gradients, regression, fitting, or automated parameter-search algorithm was used in the Astra arm.

A fresh gpt-6-astra context worked sequentially through Pendulum, Hopper, and Walker2d. Knowledge could transfer within that sequence. Candidate selection used complete-episode mean development return, with the initial random policy also eligible. Only after editing froze did the parent evaluate the selected policies on 100 shared test resets, seeds 41000 through 41099.

SAC used the native CleanRL settings, including 5,000 warmup transitions, and ran on RTX 6000 Ada GPUs. It was neither tuned nor converged at these short budgets. The comparison matches initial actors and online interaction counts, not compute or prior knowledge.

SAC wall times at the matched checkpoints were 52, 223, and 160 seconds. Astra model inference time and billing are unavailable through the runner. Candidate evaluation time excludes reasoning and cannot support a speedup claim. This report adds no new policy evaluations or training runs.

Next experiments

  1. Isolate the value of experience: compare full physics source plus feedback, task semantics plus feedback, and static information without feedback. Include a classical-controller baseline.
  2. Repeat across at least five initialization/training seeds and independent editor contexts. Report forward speed, survival, and control cost alongside return.
  3. Test hidden dynamics perturbations, then whether weight programming accelerates subsequent SAC, counting all editor feedback in the total interaction budget.
